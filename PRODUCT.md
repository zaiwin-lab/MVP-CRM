# Product

## Register

product

## Users

The staff of a small office/agency who manage leads and clients day to day — a
mix of roles (owner, sales, admin) with **varying technical comfort**. They open
KOBIS Connect many times a day to look someone up, log a follow-up, or check
what's due. Clarity and obviousness matter more than raw density, but the people
who live in it all day should never feel slowed down. Everyone shares one live
database, so the interface is a shared source of truth, not a personal scratchpad.

## Product Purpose

A simple, shared CRM: contacts & companies, tasks/follow-ups, and a dashboard
with KPIs and an activity feed. It exists to keep a small team on the same page
about who they're talking to and what happens next — replacing scattered
spreadsheets and memory. Success is that someone can find a contact and log a
next step in seconds, and that nothing important falls through the cracks.

## Brand Personality

Sharp and efficient, but warm at the edges. Three words: **precise, quick,
human.** The voice is plain and direct (a colleague, not a corporation) — it
tells you what's due and what to do without ceremony. It should feel like a
well-made tool that respects the user's time, with just enough warmth that a
non-technical teammate feels invited rather than intimidated.

## Anti-references

- **Legacy enterprise CRM** (Salesforce, SugarCRM): walls of tabs, gray density,
  tiny controls, settings everywhere. The opposite of what we want.
- **Flashy startup dashboard**: gradient heroes, giant vanity metrics, marketing
  flair. This is a work tool, not a pitch deck.
- **Childish / cartoonish apps**: candy colors, oversized rounded bubbles,
  emoji-driven UI. It has to stay credible for client-facing work.
- **Sterile corporate/bank software**: faceless, cold, humorless. Some warmth
  and humanity is required, not optional.

## Design Principles

1. **Fast by default.** The tool keeps up with the user — minimal clicks,
   keyboard-reachable actions, instant optimistic feedback. Never make someone
   wait on choreography to do their job.
2. **Obvious over clever.** A mixed-skill team should never wonder what to do
   next. Standard affordances, plain labels, one clear primary action per view.
3. **Calm density.** Show real information without noise. Let hierarchy,
   spacing, and weight do the work so a full screen still reads as quiet.
4. **Human, not corporate.** Warmth lives in tone, empty states, and small
   details — never in cartoonish color or gimmicks.
5. **Earned familiarity.** Match the quality bar of the tools people already
   trust (Linear, Stripe, Notion). No invented controls for standard tasks; the
   interface should disappear into the work.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target **WCAG 2.1 AA**. Because the team spans technical comfort levels and
uses this all day: body text ≥4.5:1 contrast (no light-gray-on-tint), visible
keyboard focus on every interactive element, hit targets comfortable for
non-power-users, and full `prefers-reduced-motion` support (motion conveys
state, never decoration). Status must never be communicated by color alone —
pair it with a label or icon.
